
     >>>>>>>>>>>>>>>> FRAME >>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>
    A frame is one rendered image of the game.
    60 FPS(frame per second) is 60 images per second

    Target frame rate = 1000 milliseconds / FPS (or 1 second / FPS)
    e.g. t_f_r = 1000 / 60 = 16.67 ms
    e.g. t_f_r = 1000 / 30 = 22.22 ms

    i.e. At 60 FPS, we have approximately 16.67 milliseconds to process input,
    update the world, and render a frame.

     >>>> Instance vs Duration >>>>>>>>>>>>>>
     Instant = stopwatch starting point.
     Duration = time measured by the stopwatch.

     One terminology correction: Instant::now() records a point in time;
     .elapsed() measures the duration since that point.


     >>>>>> Delta Time and Frame-Independent Movement >>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>
    -> Problem:
       Imagine Kai moves 5 pixels every frame.

       Case1: At 30 FPS -> 5 X 30 = 150 pixels/second
       Case2: At 60 FPS -> 5 X 60 = 300 pixels/second
       Case3: At 120 FPS -> 5 X 120 = 600 pixels/second

       That would mean the character moves four times faster on a 120 FPS computer
       than on a 30 FPS computer!
